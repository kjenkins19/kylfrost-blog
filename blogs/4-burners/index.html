Breaking the 4 Burner Theory - how to rework it for better Work Place Culture

March 7, 2026

Background

I recently came upon a concept called the "4 Burner Theory." Imagine a stove with 4 gas burners, each representing a different aspect of our lives:

  • Friends
  • Family
  • Health
  • Work

The theory is simple: to be highly successful, high-performing, you have to cut off at least one burner.

As we look around at our current culture of major CEOs and billionaires, you see a lack of family involvement or friends. When I started looking around my own life of friends, co-workers, and myself, I see the same pattern.

The Workplace Culture Problem

I began to think about this from a Workplace Culture perspective. I looked around at our Directors and up, and how they got into those roles, I realized that they had to at least significantly reduce one burner to get to where they are. Is this really what we want to promote? Is this really the best example of a conscious business?

I then began to look at myself. I unintentionally cut off the family burner 18 years ago. I have intentionally kept it that way by focusing that much more on work and friends, making it near impossible for me to find time to visit unless I am traveling for work nearby. I simply never made it a priority.

By doing this, most have always seen me as a hard worker, putting a lot of time into the community and the company.

The Global Culture Problem

There was a shift a few decades ago where we suddenly had dual-income families, and over time that became the norm. The economy itself stabilized around that, now causing that "work burner" to take up two individuals worth of time.
